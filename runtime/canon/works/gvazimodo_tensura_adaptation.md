# «Гвазимодо» — Tensura-world adaptation canon

T+160, Dwargon hall. Arlequino begins orally retelling/adapting his manuscript «Гвазимодо» for the students while Rena, Fira, Tia and Mayra are away retrieving the physical manuscript.

## Hard adaptation rules
- The work is an in-world fictional tragedy inspired structurally by Arlequino's source material but rewritten for the Tensura setting.
- Main figure: Гвазимодо / Quasimodo-equivalent is a **goblin**, visibly non-human.
- The religious institution is the **Western Holy Church / Западная церковь** of this world.
- Central social conflict: the Church and much of the surrounding human society despise/fear Gvazimodo specifically because he is a monster/non-human, despite the fact that he has lived most of his life in the church's own bell tower.
- Do not import medieval France, Catholicism, Romani ethnicity, Paris, or real-world doctrine literally. Translate the functions of those elements into the Tensura world.
- The city may be fictional within Arlequino's work so the play does not assert false historical facts about real nations.

## Character/function mapping
- **Гвазимодо**: goblin foundling; physically deformed; raised/hidden in the bell tower of a great Western Church basilica; bell-ringer. The church below preaches human purity and hostility toward monsters. He can live in the old upper tower but avoids consecrated rites below because holy power is painful to him. The basilica is simultaneously his home, refuge, prison and the institution that considers his species impure.
- **Клод Фролло**: senior human cleric/scholar of the Western Church. He took the abandoned goblin in when young, but treats him less as an equal person than as a secret, dependent possession and proof of his own authority. Public doctrine and private obsession increasingly split him apart.
- **Эсмеральда**: human street dancer/singer raised among wandering performers and people living outside church respectability. She is not hated for race; she is targeted as an outsider associated with demi-humans/monsters and accused of corrupting respectable humans. Her kindness to Gvazimodo is one of the first times a human treats him simply as a person.
- **Феб**: handsome human officer/captain in the city guard or church-aligned security, admired by Esmeralda but emotionally shallow and unreliable.
- **Гренгуар**: poor human poet/playwright surviving among the city's performers and outcasts.
- **Клопен / Court-of-Miracles function**: leader of a hidden mixed quarter of beggars, performers, fugitives, poor humans and some tolerated/hidden non-humans. They are not a literal Romani analogue.

## Plot translation
1. Great human city under strong Western Church influence celebrates a public religious/civic festival.
2. Gvazimodo, the goblin bell-ringer, is mocked and treated as a monster even while the crowd delights in his grotesque appearance.
3. Frollo's obsession with Esmeralda begins. He orders/manipulates Gvazimodo into trying to seize her.
4. Gvazimodo is captured and publicly punished for 'monster violence'; Frollo abandons him to protect his own standing.
5. Esmeralda gives the punished goblin water. This becomes the emotional hinge of Gvazimodo's loyalty.
6. Frollo attacks/stabs Phoebus and Esmeralda is framed for the crime, with church prejudice and accusations of monster-tainted sorcery/heresy making the accusation believable to authorities.
7. Gvazimodo rescues Esmeralda from execution and carries her into the basilica/bell tower, claiming sanctuary even though the institution itself denies a monster's moral equality.
8. The outcast quarter later attempts to rescue Esmeralda. Gvazimodo initially mistakes them for attackers and defends the tower against the very people trying to help her.
9. Frollo exploits the chaos, betrays Esmeralda and delivers her to death when she rejects him.
10. Gvazimodo sees what happened, kills/throws Frollo from the tower, then disappears. The ending preserves the tragedy of the rejected goblin choosing death beside the only person who once showed him uncomplicated kindness.

## Themes specific to this world
- 'Monster' as a religious/political category versus personhood proven by action.
- A church building as both sacred refuge and machinery of exclusion.
- Human purity doctrine contrasted with the moral ugliness of the humans enforcing it.
- Gvazimodo's non-human body is not the source of the tragedy; the tragedy is what humans decide that body means.
- Esmeralda's kindness matters because she does not 'civilize' or 'redeem' him; she recognizes a person already there.
