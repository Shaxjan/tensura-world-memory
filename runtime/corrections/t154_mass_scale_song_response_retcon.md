# T+154 — AUTHORITATIVE RETCON: mass-scale response to Arlequino's war song

Context: During evacuation of the Eurazania capital after Milim's declaration of war on Carrion, Arlequino performs his adapted «Страна огромная / Священная война» with magical resonance amplification while moving through the evacuation column.

Correction:
- Prior descriptions that only a few dozen people were singing are underscaled and superseded.
- The capital is evacuating on a mass scale. Arlequino deliberately amplifies the song with resonance so it carries across large sections of the evacuation flow.
- This song functions as a simple, repetitive wartime mobilization anthem at exactly the psychological moment for which such songs are effective: immediate external threat, collective fear, loyalty to homeland and ruler, and a militarized beastman culture.
- Therefore the immediate response should be mass, not niche: hundreds can join the refrain in the first large wave of direct listeners; as the song is repeated and carried down the moving columns, separate blocks of evacuees and soldiers can answer in large groups, with the refrain spreading to thousands over the continuing evacuation if acoustically exposed.
- Not everyone instantly knows every verse. Full verses remain unevenly known after one or two hearings, but the refrain and opening lines are extremely easy to learn and can become a rolling chant across the column.
- Civilians do not all abandon evacuation to fight. The song transforms panic into disciplined defiance, mutual aid, pride, and determination to survive/return; soldiers and military-age beastmen react more aggressively.
- Exact total capital population remains unspecified unless established elsewhere; do not invent a fixed city population. Describe scale proportionally to a capital-wide evacuation and the reach of magical resonance.
- Rena and the world should recognize that this is not merely a provocative personal performance but the spontaneous birth of a wartime anthem with potentially national reach.

This correction has priority over previous low-count crowd-response descriptions of the same scene.
